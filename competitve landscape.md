# Indie RTS Competitive Dossier: Pricing, Revenue, DLC & Go-to-Market (July 2026)

## TL;DR
- **The indie RTS space rewards three distinct, proven business models:** premium-price + long DLC/free-update treadmill (Northgard, 5M+ units official), low-price high-volume minimalism (Thronefall, 1M+ units official at ~$7-13), and free/open-source community-donation (Mindustry). All three are profitable; the common thread is a tight, streamable core loop plus years of post-launch support.
- **Mac is real but tiny and under-served:** Northgard, Thronefall, Bad North, and Mindustry all run natively on macOS, but the biggest Windows comparables (They Are Billions, Cataclismo, Tempest Rising) have NO native Mac build. macOS is only ~1-2% of Steam users, yet ~82% of those are now Apple Silicon — a homogeneous, capable target that Apple's Game Porting Toolkit is making cheaper to reach.
- **Lifetime revenue leaders (all third-party estimates unless flagged official):** They Are Billions (~$26-44M gross, ~2.3M+ copies estimated), Northgard (5M+ units official milestone; ~$28-36M Steam gross estimated), Mindustry (~$9.7M Steam gross estimated despite being free everywhere else), Bad North (~$8M gross, ~$2.4M net estimated), Thronefall (1M+ copies official; ~$4.4M estimated Steam gross), Tempest Rising (~$5.7-10.9M gross estimated).

## Key Findings

**1. Model diversity beats genre orthodoxy.** The commercially successful indie RTS titles rarely play like classic StarCraft-style RTS. They are RTS-hybrids: city-builder/4X (Northgard), minimalist tower-defense/strategy (Thronefall, Bad North), or factory/tower-defense (Mindustry). The one "pure" classic-RTS revivalist, Tempest Rising, is a solid but smaller performer despite a strong pedigree and the highest price in the set.

**2. DLC monetization splits sharply.** Northgard runs the textbook "DLC treadmill" — 11+ paid clan packs at $4.99 each plus a paid expansion, all layered on top of continuous free content updates and periodic re-releases (Definitive Edition). Thronefall, They Are Billions, Bad North, and Mindustry took the opposite path: free content updates only, with at most a cosmetic/soundtrack upsell.

**3. Early Access and streamer-driven launches are the dominant go-to-market.** They Are Billions, Thronefall, Tempest Rising, and Cataclismo all used Steam Early Access. Thronefall's launch is the clearest documented case study of engineered virality via YouTube/Twitch.

**4. Publisher backing varies widely** — from fully self-published (Numantian's They Are Billions, Anuken's Mindustry) to boutique-publisher-backed (Bad North/Raw Fury, Cataclismo/Hooded Horse, Tempest Rising/3D Realms+Knights Peak) to VC-funded-but-independent (Shiro Games).

## Details

### Northgard (Shiro Games) — the premium + DLC-treadmill benchmark
- **Developer/Publisher:** Shiro Games (Bordeaux, France), self-published. Founded 2012 by Nicolas Cannasse and Sébastien Vidal.
- **Ownership:** Independent. Raised €50M (~$48.5M) on October 11, 2022 from Cathay Capital Private Equity (Cathay's first games investment), valuing the studio at €150M (~$145.6M). Per Cathay's official release, "the founders... Nicolas Cannasse and Sebastien Vidal continue the adventure as majority shareholders, keeping Shiro Games independent." **NOT owned by Tencent** — the Tencent relationship is limited to Tencent Cloud infrastructure for Asian servers (for Darksburg/Northgard). Studio employs ~60 people and runs a third-party publishing label, Shiro Unlimited (launched 2021/2023). Game Seer Venture Partners is also listed as an investor.
- **Release history:** Steam Early Access early 2017; full PC launch March 7, 2018. Later to PS4/Xbox One/Switch (Sept-Oct 2019), iOS (April 13, 2021, via Playdigious), Android (Aug 2021). "Definitive Edition" re-release on Steam December 4-5, 2025.
- **Platforms incl. Mac:** Windows, macOS, Linux natively on Steam; also available historically via MacGameStore. iOS/Android via Playdigious.
- **Pricing:** Definitive Edition base $34.99 (Steam). Originally launched around $29.99. Deep, frequent discounts — routinely 60-72% off; recent sale price $9.79-$13.99; historical low $2.99 (Dec 4, 2024). iOS app $7.99. DLC clans $4.99 each; mobile Scale Bundle ~$6.99.
- **DLC catalog:** 11+ paid clan DLCs at $4.99 each (Snake, Dragon, Horse, Kraken, Ox, Lynx, Squirrel, Rat, Eagle, Stoat, Owl, Hounds, Turtle/Hafgufa, Lion/Neustria), bundled into "Scale Bundle" and "Fur Bundle," plus a paid Cross of Vidar expansion pack and an OST. This paid layer sits on top of a long list of FREE major updates (Map Editor, Conquest mode, Kröwns & Daggers 2021, Sword & Solace 2022, Trials of Odin 2023, Bifröst 2024). Cosmetics are earned in-game only, never paid.
- **Sales (official milestones):** 500k (March 2018) → 2M (Sept 2020) → 2.4M (2022) → 4M (7-year anniversary, 2025) → **"more than 5MM units across various platforms"** (official Dec 5, 2025 press release). Cannasse cited "over 3 million units" in Oct 2022.
- **Revenue (estimates):** games-stats ~$36M; Raijin ~$28.4M Steam gross / ~1.4M Steam copies (medium confidence). SteamSpy owners 2-5M.
- **Reviews/players:** ~54,000-68,000 Steam reviews at 85% positive ("Very Positive"). Current concurrent ~1,400-2,080; all-time peak ~20,198.
- **Engine:** Shiro's in-house Heaps.io / Haxe engine (Cannasse is the creator of the Haxe language).

### Thronefall (Grizzly Games → Mythwright) — the low-price, high-volume minimalist model
- **Developer:** Grizzly Games (Berlin), a two-person team: Jonas Tyroller (also a game-dev YouTuber) and Paul Schnepf. Self-published; IP sold to Mythwright Games (Feb 11, 2025) for continued development.
- **Release:** Steam Early Access August 2, 2023; full 1.0 launch October 11, 2024. Also Nintendo Switch.
- **Platforms incl. Mac:** Windows and macOS (native, macOS Ventura+), Steam Deck Verified.
- **Pricing:** Launched ~$7 in EA; current standard price $12.99 (was ~$8.44 at points). Deliberately low price as a marketing lever.
- **DLC:** None paid — monetization is base game + free content updates. Final 2.11 update (12 new mini-modes: True Endless, Reverse Siege, etc.) shipped to celebrate the 1M milestone, after which Tyroller stated the content was "officially complete."
- **Sales:** 315,000 units / ~$1.5M gross by ~Aug 2023 — per GameDiscoverCo, dev-reported "315,000 units on Steam already, with a return rate of 6.9% and gross revenue of slightly more than $1.5 million USD." **Over 1 million copies sold** (official, announced Feb 3, 2025). ~$4.4M estimated Steam gross (Raijin, 562k Steam copies, medium confidence); VG Insights ~698k paid units.
- **Reviews:** 18,000+ reviews at 96% positive ("Very/Overwhelmingly Positive").
- **Marketing (best-documented case study):** Tyroller and Schnepf attribute success to (a) an ultra-tight, two-sentence-describable concept ("build by day, defend by night"); (b) a large streamer/YouTube push at launch — Schnepf described it as "a mix of everything - small and big influencers as well as some more traditional press outlets," crediting outreach agency Future Friends and streamer Sodapoppin; (c) an engineered speedrun contest and viral video; (d) leveraging Tyroller's own YouTube channel for wishlists (which "took off like a rocket" pre-launch). Regional surprises noted in Korea/China. Won the 2023 German Developer Award (Best Casual Game).

### Mindustry (Anuken) — the free / open-source community model
- **Developer:** Anuke (Anuken), effectively a solo developer. Free and open-source under GNU GPL v3; source on GitHub; fully moddable, no ads or IAP.
- **Release:** 2017 (itch.io/free); Steam release September 26, 2019.
- **Platforms incl. Mac:** Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS. Free on all except iOS (paid, due to Apple developer fees).
- **Pricing:** Free to download from the developer; $9.99 on Steam (buyers pay for convenience — achievements, Workshop, seamless multiplayer, and to support the dev). Development funded by donations.
- **DLC:** None. All content updates free.
- **Revenue (estimate):** ~$9.7M Steam gross / ~$2.86M net estimated (Steam Revenue Calculator) — remarkable for a game that is free everywhere else. Essentially voluntary "tip jar" revenue from players who could have played free.
- **Reviews/players:** ~25,900-27,600 Steam reviews at ~95% positive ("Overwhelmingly Positive"). Concurrent ~700-2,000; peaks ~3,300-4,300.
- **Takeaway:** Proof that open-source + genuine quality + an optional paid Steam SKU can generate multi-million-dollar revenue while maximizing reach and goodwill.

### Bad North (Plausible Concept / Raw Fury) — minimalist real-time tactics
- **Developer:** Plausible Concept (Malmö, Sweden; Oskar Stålberg and Richard Meredith). **Publisher:** Raw Fury (Stockholm).
- **Publisher ownership:** Raw Fury is majority-owned by Altor Equity Partners (acquired August 2021).
- **Release:** Switch/PS4/Xbox Aug 2018; Windows Oct 16, 2018; mobile Oct 2019. Free "Jotunn Edition" expansion July 2019 (became the definitive version).
- **Platforms incl. Mac:** Windows and macOS (native), plus consoles and iOS/Android. Steam Deck Verified.
- **Pricing:** $14.99 base; very deep discounts — routinely 80-84% off ($2.34-$2.99). Mobile revenue now negligible (~$7k/month).
- **DLC:** Jotunn Edition was a FREE content expansion. The only paid add-on is a "Deluxe Edition Upgrade" (cosmetic/OST). Model = base game + free content.
- **Revenue (estimate):** ~$8.1M Steam gross / ~$2.4M net (Steam Revenue Calculator); games-stats ~$3.5M. SteamSpy owners 500k-1M.
- **Reviews:** ~11,000-12,000 Steam reviews at 93% positive. Strong Simplified Chinese review share (~2,864 reviews) — a notable China-market presence.

### They Are Billions (Numantian Games) — Windows-only revenue leader [NO MAC]
- **Developer/Publisher:** Numantian Games (Spain), self-published (console ports by BlitWorks).
- **Release:** Steam Early Access December 13, 2017; full 1.0 with the free "New Empire" campaign June 18, 2019. PS4/Xbox One July 2019.
- **Platforms:** Windows only on PC — **NO native macOS build (confirmed)**. Consoles via BlitWorks.
- **Pricing:** EA launched ~$24.99; current standard $29.99; typical discounts 10-30%.
- **DLC:** The 48-mission "New Empire" campaign shipped FREE with 1.0. Only paid DLC is a Soundtrack & Art Book (~$6.99) — no paid gameplay expansions.
- **Sales/revenue (estimates diverge widely):** Raijin ~2.3M copies / ~$44.5M Steam gross; games-stats ~$26M; SteamSpy owners 2-5M. Officially, "over 400,000 copies in early release" during EA (early 2018) — a genuine EA breakout hit that topped Steam and Humble charts. Flag: the $26M vs $44.5M spread illustrates how unreliable third-party estimates are.
- **Reviews:** ~45,600 Steam reviews; ~80% positive (English filter) / ~84-85% (all languages), "Very Positive." Metacritic 77.

### Tempest Rising (Slipgate Ironworks + 2B Games / 3D Realms + Knights Peak) — classic-RTS revival [NO NATIVE MAC]
- **Developer:** Slipgate Ironworks and 2B Games. **Publisher:** 3D Realms and Knights Peak (My.Games). Composer Frank Klepacki (of Command & Conquer fame).
- **Release:** Full launch April 17, 2025. Engine: Unreal Engine 5.
- **Platforms:** Windows only — no native macOS (players report running it via CrossOver/D3DMetal on Apple Silicon). A Mac version is frequently requested but not released.
- **Pricing:** $39.99 (the highest base price in this set).
- **Revenue (estimates):** Steam Revenue Calculator ~$10.9M gross / ~$3.2M net; Raijin ~239k copies / ~$5.7M gross (medium confidence); VG Insights ~420k paid units. GameSensor: ~10k buyers / ~$500k in July 2025 alone.
- **Positioning:** A deliberate throwback to 1990s-2000s Command & Conquer, notable for reviving the "pure" classic RTS formula that most indie hits avoid. Solid but mid-sized commercially.

### Cataclismo (Digital Sun / Hooded Horse) — RTS tower-defense [NO NATIVE MAC]
- **Developer:** Digital Sun (Spain, makers of Moonlighter). **Publisher:** Hooded Horse (Digital Sun parted with originally-announced publisher Humble Games in Sept 2023; a Kickstarter in Oct 2023 funded within a week). Engine: Unity.
- **Release:** Steam Early Access July 22, 2024 (300k+ wishlists, 20% launch discount through Aug 4, 2024); full 1.0 March 20, 2025.
- **Platforms:** Windows only — no native macOS (CrossOver-playable).
- **Pricing:** $29.99 base; discounts to $14.99-$23.99; historical low ~$12.72.
- **Reviews:** ~3,300 Steam reviews, ~89-90% positive ("Very Positive"). Metacritic 82.
- **Revenue (estimate):** games-stats ~$1.9M — a modest performer relative to They Are Billions, reflecting its newness and niche.

### The Mac RTS niche
- **macOS is ~1-2% of Steam users** (Steam Hardware Survey — official but self-selected and volatile: Oct 2025 macOS 2.11% / Linux 3.05%; March 2026 macOS 2.35% / Linux 5.33%; a Feb 2025 dip to under 1% per It's FOSS). Notably, Linux has overtaken macOS on Steam.
- **But the Mac base is now homogeneous and capable:** ~82% of Macs on Steam are Apple Silicon (Valve's May 2025 survey: "VirtualApple" chips = 81.69%), a far better porting target than the fragmented Intel era.
- **Apple's Game Porting Toolkit (GPTK)** — introduced 2023, now GPTK 2/3/4 (2025-26) — is actively lowering the cost/time of bringing Windows games to Mac/iPad/iPhone as a "unified gaming platform."
- **The under-served thesis is well-supported:** commentators describe a chicken-and-egg cycle ("developers don't port because the audience looks small; the audience stays small because the games don't arrive"). Every major Windows RTS comparable in this report (They Are Billions, Cataclismo, Tempest Rising) is Windows-only — a concrete illustration of the gap. The indie hits that DO ship Mac builds (Northgard, Thronefall, Bad North, Mindustry) capture a small but real, low-competition audience.

## Recommendations

**For a developer/publisher entering the indie RTS space (staged plan):**
1. **Pick a hybrid, not a pure RTS.** The data is decisive: city-builder/tower-defense/4X hybrids (Northgard, Thronefall, Mindustry, TAB) massively outsell classic-RTS revivals. Pure C&C-style (Tempest Rising) tops out in the mid-single-digit millions despite a strong pedigree and $39.99 price.
2. **Choose one of the three proven monetization models deliberately:**
   - *Premium + treadmill (Northgard):* only viable if you can commit to 5+ years of paid DLC + free updates. Threshold to change course: if paid-DLC attach rate or concurrent players decay faster than one clan/expansion cycle can rebuild them, pivot to a Definitive-Edition re-bundle (as Shiro did in Dec 2025).
   - *Low-price volume (Thronefall):* price $7-13, keep the loop describable in two sentences, and treat streamer virality as the primary channel. Threshold: if you cannot demonstrate strong pre-launch wishlist velocity from influencer/YouTube coverage, delay launch.
   - *Free/OSS (Mindustry):* only if you can sustain on donations + an optional paid Steam SKU; maximizes reach and community goodwill but requires a solo/very-lean cost base.
3. **Use Early Access + a demo, and engineer streamer coverage.** Every EA title here benefited. Copy Thronefall's playbook: two-sentence hook, demo during Steam Next Fest, coordinated influencer outreach (consider an agency like Future Friends), and a launch-window "event" (speedrun contest, viral clip).
4. **Ship a native Mac build if your engine allows it cheaply (Unity/Godot/custom Metal).** Mac is only ~1-2% of Steam but is nearly uncontested for RTS — Northgard/Thronefall/Bad North/Mindustry all serve it while the biggest Windows comparables don't. Use Apple's GPTK to evaluate feasibility first. Threshold to justify the port: if GPTK testing shows acceptable Apple Silicon performance and porting cost is under ~5% of budget, do it; if it requires a major engine rework, skip native and simply ensure CrossOver-friendliness (as Tempest Rising does).
5. **Build community infrastructure early** — Discord, Steam Workshop/modding, and (for the OSS model) an open GitHub. Moddability (Northgard map editor, Mindustry OSS, TAB Workshop) consistently extends the sales tail.

**Thresholds that would change the recommendation:** If Apple materially grows Mac's Steam share above ~5% or launches a first-party storefront push, native Mac becomes a priority rather than a nice-to-have. If a classic-RTS revival breaks out commercially (a Tempest Rising sequel or a new entrant exceeding ~1M units), reconsider the "hybrids only" guidance.

## Caveats
- **All revenue and copies-sold figures are third-party ESTIMATES unless explicitly labeled "official."** Estimates from Gamalytic, VG Insights (Sensor Tower), Raijin, games-stats, Steam Revenue Calculator, and SteamSpy diverge substantially — e.g., They Are Billions is estimated at both ~$26M and ~$44.5M; Tempest Rising at ~$5.7M and ~$10.9M. Treat all as order-of-magnitude only.
- **Official milestones** (Northgard 5M+ units, Thronefall 1M+ copies, TAB 400k+ in EA) are developer/publisher announcements and generally reliable, but "units across all platforms" is not directly comparable to Steam-only estimates.
- **Prices fluctuate constantly** with Steam sales; figures here are July 2026 snapshots. Verify live on Steam/Mac App Store before quoting.
- **Steam macOS share is volatile** (self-selected survey; has swung between under 1% and ~2.35% across 2025-26). The "9.8% of global gamers" macOS figure sometimes cited (Pingle Studio) uses a different, non-Steam denominator and should not be compared directly.
- **Recommended primary sources for verification:** SteamDB (prices/players/history), Steam Hardware Survey (platform share), official developer/publisher press releases and X/Twitter (sales milestones), the GameDiscoverCo newsletter deep-dive on Thronefall, Jonas Tyroller's game-dev podcast/interviews (Thronefall go-to-market), Cathay Capital's Oct 2022 release and Les Echos / Game World Observer (Shiro Games €50M funding), and Games Press (Northgard Definitive Edition/5M announcement).
